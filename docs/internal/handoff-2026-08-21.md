The hole. There are three ways a Bash command reaches the red zone: naming the file,

The Makefile was left alone, and that turned out to be the better half of the trade.

file that `make fmt` does not fix, which is worse than what it solves. The hook sits in
Claude's Bash path and not in your terminal, so `make fmt` still formats everything

naming files, and `gofmt -l .` is read only and still allowed.

This is the failure the hook's own header names as the worst one. A guardrail that

Two parts, both inside the hook:

1. A heredoc body is DATA. Scan `git commit -F - <<MSG` up to the `<<` and no

   not a sandbox.

### Closed 24 August 2026, and the closing bought P2-6e

Al applied the patch in the working tree and instructed Claude to commit it, which is
the confirmation the note in item 16 was asking for. It is committed on its own, and
separately from the repair below, because a loosening and its repair should be two
entries in the history rather than one. The commit message says whose patch it is.

**It was probed before being committed rather than after, and it opened eight
routes.** That is P2-6e, it is Claude's, and it is fixed in the commit that follows.
The finding is not that the patch was careless. It is that changing WHAT COUNTS AS A
COMMAND touches three rules that all read that answer, and two of the eight were the
ordinary form of a command rather than an exotic one:

1. **A multi-line command.** The hook collapsed newlines to SPACES, under a comment
   claiming that could only make it refuse more. True until the command-position
   anchor existed, then exactly backwards: line two was glued to the end of line one,
   so its verb was no longer at the start of anything. `cd /tmp` on line one walked a
   `sed -i` of `compute.go` straight through, and so did `make fmt`. Newlines are
   semicolons now, which is what a newline means.
2. **Truncating at `<<` threw the redirect away.** `cat <<'EOF' > compute.go` puts the
   target AFTER the marker, so cutting the line there removed the thing the redirect
   rule reads. Only the heredoc BODY is dropped now, and the line that opens it is
   scanned like any other command.

The remaining six are the same mistake in smaller shapes: a command position is not
always the first word of a line. `FOO=1 sed -i`, `sudo`, `env`, `time`, `nohup`,
`xargs`, and the `then` of a compound statement each put the verb one word later, and
each walked through. The anchor skips those prefixes now.

**What the repair cost, stated rather than buried.** A multi-line `git commit -m`
whose message quotes a zone path on its own line is refused again, because a newline
is now a separator and the quoted line reads as a command. The escape is the one that
part 1 exists to provide: `git commit -F -` with a heredoc, whose body is data and is
not scanned. Prose belongs in a heredoc body or a file, and the command line is for
commands.

**Both directions are proven on every run.** P2-6e is thirty-six probes in the two
harnesses that produced it and seven in the script, and it was tested by putting Al's
patch back on its own, where it reads PROVEN, and then restoring the repair, where it
reads NOT. P2-6b gained two multi-line probes in the same pass, because once a newline

## 5b. What the golden fixture should call itself

**Why it is not Claude's.** It is a claim about what the data is, and it sits inside
the fixture the whole methodology is checked against.

`GoldenSnapshot()` labels itself `DataSourceHorizon`. The contract and DEC-003

**And neither label is exactly right, which is the interesting part.** The

executed". This book was reconstructed from `manage_sell_offer` and `manage_buy_offer`

**How to resolve, pick one.**

- Broaden the enum description so `trades-implied` covers any reconstruction from

- Add a fourth value, something like `offers-implied`, and say why it ranks above
  `trades-implied` in confidence.
- Keep `horizon` on the fixture and accept the label is loose, which is the one option
  that leaves a reader misinformed.

The first is cheapest and honest. The second is the most accurate and costs a contract
change plus a mocks regeneration.

### Answered 23 August 2026: `offers-implied`, the second option

The reason the first option was rejected is worth keeping. Broadening `trades-implied` to
cover any reconstruction from ledger operations would leave its own warning false for half

that was consumed. A label carrying a warning that is untrue for some of its members is
worse than one more label.

**The design smell is recorded rather than fixed.** `DataSource` squashes two axes into

## 13. `OracleResistance` went back to a scalar

**Added 23 August 2026.** The cross-check at the end of this document printed
`unaccounted: P1-6`, which is what it exists to do. This item is the required entry.

**What happened.** The 1.0.3 rewrite of `internal/domain/types.go` replaced the
`OracleResistance` struct with `OracleResistance *decimal.Decimal`, and changed the
quantity along with the shape. Methodology section 8 now defines
`MR = MC_orderbookOnly(P_target) + V_genuine(W)`, a sum, where the field it replaced
held a ratio plus the two inputs the ratio was computed from.

**Why it is not Claude's.** DEC-003 section 8.2 closed this in favour of the object,
and the reasoning was not about taste. A ratio has two states that a scalar cannot
carry: `GenuineVolume` of zero makes it undefined, and a ratio built on a
`ManipulationCost` whose `Reachable` is false is a meaningless number. The object made
both readable and set `Ratio` to nil. Reversing a recorded decision, and redefining
the metric from a ratio to a sum, is a methodology judgment.

**One thing worth noticing about the sum.** It is not the same measurement. A ratio
below 1 says the attack is cheaper than the genuine trading it has to hide inside. A
sum says how much capital the whole defence represents, and it no longer answers the
question the metric was named for. Both are defensible quantities. They are not
interchangeable, and only one of them is what `oracleResistance` means in the frozen
contract and in five mock responses.

**How to resolve, pick one.**

- Keep the object and the ratio. Revert the field, keep the `orderbookOnly` term in
  the numerator, which is the genuine improvement inside this change. Nothing in the
  contract moves.
- Keep the sum and rename it. It is a different metric, so give it a different name
  and say in section 8 why the ratio was dropped. Costs a contract change, a mocks
  regeneration, and a `MethodologyVersion` note.

**And a note on how this was found.** The check for P1-6 matched the exact column
alignment of the old struct field, so when the scalar came back with different spacing
the check went green and reported the defect gone. That is the fifth check in this
repository defeated by the thing it was checking, and the first that produced a false
negative rather than a false positive. It now matches the declaration instead of its
layout.

### Answered 23 August 2026: keep the object, and put the sum inside it

Both quantities are kept, because they are not two shapes of one number. The ratio asks
whether manipulation is cheap relative to the market it has to hide inside, and it is
dimensionless, so it can rank fifty assets. The sum asks how much capital an attack on an
averaging oracle needs in total, and it is in the quote asset, so it can only be read
against one pair, which is open question Q7 all over again.

`OracleResistance` is an object again, with `CriticalDelta`, `ManipulationCost`,
`Reachable`, `GenuineVolume`, `WindowSeconds`, `Ratio`, and now `TotalAttackCost`. The
numerator keeps the genuine improvement inside the 1.0.3 change, `orderbookOnly` rather
than `combined`.

The four arguments that settled it, in the order they mattered:

1. **Two undefined states have to stay readable.** A `GenuineVolume` of zero is a
   high-value finding, not missing data, and a scalar sum reports `MC + 0 = MC`, an
   ordinary looking number with the finding erased. A cost to an unreachable target is not
   a cost, and a scalar has nowhere to carry `Reachable`. That second one is the same bug
   the `δ_critical = 0.5` decision was taken to avoid, reintroduced one layer up.
2. **The window is still an assumption.** `WindowSeconds` sits inside the object, so every
   archived response records which window produced it. When Reflector's real window is
   finally confirmed, that is the difference between knowing what to recompute and
   guessing. For a metric whose key input is a guess, this is the strongest argument of
   the four.
3. **Auditability.** The object can be archived as a complete record and recomputed by an
   outsider. The scalar carries none of its inputs.
4. **Cost.** The object costs nothing: contract, mocks, and schema all already hold it.

`TotalAttackCost` is documented as a LOWER BOUND, because needing exactly `GenuineVolume`
of extra volume to dominate a VWAP is an approximation. What is really required depends on
the weighting and on where in the window the attack lands. That belongs in
`06-oracle-resilience.md` section 1 as an assumption, not as an identity, and it is not
written there yet.

## 18. Three gaps in the API contract, found by implementing it

**Added 24 August 2026, out of writing `internal/api`.**

**Why they are not Claude's.** `docs/api/` is yellow with one limit: a change bumps
the contract version and regenerates the mocks. Two of these three change what a
response MEANS rather than how it is spelled, and the frontend has already been
handed mocks. None of them blocks the handlers, which are written and tested; each
is a wording decision that then takes Claude one pass to execute.

**1. The error enum has no code for two real states.** `ASSET_NOT_MONITORED` now
carries two conditions that are not the same thing, told apart only by their
message:

| State | What it means | What it borrows |
|---|---|---|
| The pair is in the demonstration set but has no result yet | Ordinary today, and the state the frontend will meet first | `ASSET_NOT_MONITORED` |
| The asset is monitored against several quotes and none was named | The primary pair rule is undecided, see gap 3 | `INVALID_ASSET_ID` |

The first matters most, because "not monitored" tells a consumer to stop asking,
while "no result yet" tells them to ask again later. Two candidate codes:
`METRICS_NOT_AVAILABLE` and `QUOTE_AMBIGUOUS`. Adding a value to that enum is
additive and breaks no generated client.

**2. `X-Keel-Staleness-Seconds` is defined as something other than what its name
suggests.** The header's own description says "how many seconds behind the latest
ledger this data was WHEN IT WAS COMPUTED", which is `computedAt` minus
`ledgerClosedAt`, and that is what the handler sends. But the contract preamble
then says "The `X-Keel-Staleness-Seconds` header states by how much [the metrics
lag]. The target maximum is 900 seconds", which is a statement about NOW, not
about compute time. Those are two different quantities and the contract asserts
both.

Which one a consumer wants is the decision. A dashboard deciding whether to show
a staleness warning needs the second. An archived response being audited needs the
first. Both are cheap: the first is stored, and the second is a subtraction from
the clock at request time. The honest resolution is probably to keep this header
as the first and add a second header for the second, because renaming the existing
one silently changes what every archived response meant.

**3. The primary pair rule cannot be implemented yet, and that is item 3's
problem rather than this one's.** The `quote` parameter's description says omitting
it uses "the pair with the largest combined depth at 10 percent". That is decision
D-1, and `02-pair-selection.md` is a worksheet whose checklist says no decisions
are recorded in it yet. Until it says something, the API resolves a single pair and
refuses to guess between several. Recorded here so the contract's confident wording
is not mistaken for a rule that exists.

**One thing that is NOT a gap, and is worth writing down so it does not get
"fixed".** The contract's `/methodology` example carries `manipulationCheapUnit:
XLM` and `thinDepth5PctUnit: XLM`. The handler omits both keys. `domain.Thresholds`
holds no unit, and these thresholds are compared against notionals denominated in
each asset's own QUOTE, so emitting `XLM` would assert a unit that is wrong for
every pair not quoted in XLM. The thresholds map is documented as open ended, so
omitting a key is allowed. The underlying problem, that an absolute threshold in
one currency is compared against notionals in many, is open question Q7 and is
yours.

## 19. The holder distribution is recordable now, and nothing is recording it

**Added 24 August 2026, out of writing `internal/horizon/holders.go`.**

**What was built, so the decision is not about code.** `keel record -holders`
reads `/assets` for the issued supply and pages `/accounts` for every trustline
holder, writes `recordings/holders/{asset}/{ledgerSeq}.json.gz` with the raw
bytes beside the parsed conclusions, and never overwrites a file. Fifteen tests,
`make record-holders` for one round. It excludes nothing, ranks nothing, and
computes nothing.

**Why it is on this list rather than simply done.** Two halves, and both are
yours.

| The half | What it is | What it needs from you |
|---|---|---|
| Switching it on | It is OFF by default | A pair list and a cap, then it runs beside the recorder you already run |
| Making the numbers mean something | D-5 and D-6 | The two rules in `07-supporting-metrics.md`, which is a worksheet |

**The argument for switching it on this week, and it is the recorder's argument
in a sharper form.** An order book can be rebuilt from the operations that posted
it, which is exactly what `offers-implied` means and what makes the golden
fixture possible six months after the event. A trustline balance cannot be
rebuilt from anything Horizon serves. So holder concentration for today's ledger
is available today and available nowhere afterwards, and the loss is silent,
because the endpoint keeps answering today's question perfectly well. Hubble can
answer it historically and DEC-002 defers Hubble, which means the gap is real for
as long as that decision stands.

**What it costs, stated so the decision is informed.** A pair snapshot is three
requests regardless of the market. A holder reading is one request per two
hundred accounts, so it is the first reading in this repository whose cost grows
with the asset. The cap is `-holder-pages`, 25 pages or 5000 accounts by
default. A reading that hits the cap is written, flagged, and logged as
TRUNCATED, and it is a lower bound on the holder count that answers no
concentration question, because the account it is missing may be the largest one.

**One thing to check before you turn it on**, and it is ten minutes. Look at
USTRY's real holder count in `/assets` first. If it is above 5000 the default cap
truncates every reading, and a fortnight of truncated recordings is a fortnight
of evidence that cannot answer the metric it was taken for. Raise the cap, or
record it knowing it is a count and not a distribution, but decide it rather than
discover it in the files.

**Two things Claude deliberately did not decide, and why.** The issuer is
returned and flagged rather than filtered, because excluding it is the obvious
exclusion and still D-5's to make, and evidence that already applied the rule
cannot be re-read under a different one. And supply is read as the AUTHORIZED
balance only, with the unauthorized and authorized-to-maintain-liabilities
figures left in the raw bytes untouched, because which of the three belongs in a
volume-to-supply denominator is D-6.
