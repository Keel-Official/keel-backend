BOTH DIRECTIONS ARE NEEDED AND NEITHER IS SUFFICIENT. home_domain alone proves
only that whoever controls the account typed a domain into it, which anybody can
do. The toml alone proves only that whoever controls the domain typed an account
into it. The two together mean the account operator and the domain operator agree
about each other, which is what SEP-1 asks for.

The other six CURRENCIES blocks in that toml are ICE, dICE, governICE, upvoteICE,
downvoteICE and gdICE, all on GAXSGZ2JM3LNWOO4WRGADISNMWO4HQLG4QBGUZRKH5ZHL3EQBGX73ICE,
which is a DIFFERENT account from the AQUA issuer. Only the block whose code is
literally AQUA was read.

97 distinct assets share the AQUA ticker and 13 of them use an aqua-flavoured
domain. None of that was used to choose. Matching on the ticker, on the domain
looking plausible, or on which issuer had the most holders would each have picked
an issuer without proving anything about it. Asset identity is the pair
(code, issuer), which is exactly what the two directions above pin down.

4. WHY 1308 POOLS BECOME ONE RECORD
-------------------------------------

The 1308 figure is right. Paged through every one of them:

  reserves=AQUA:GBNZILST...   (the issuer alone, no second asset)
  -> 1308 pools in 7 pages of 200

Then every one of those 1308 was keyed by its unordered reserve asset pair:

  asset pairs appearing in MORE THAN ONE pool: 0

All 1308 are distinct asset pairs. The distribution across them:

  fee_bp : {30: 1308}
  type   : {constant_product: 1308}

So AQUA is in 1308 pools because it is paired with 1308 DIFFERENT assets, not
because any pair of assets has several pools. The `reserves=A,B` filter is an AND
over both assets, measured separately in
docs/evidences/liquidity_pools_reserves_2026-08-25.txt section 3, so it selects
the one pool for that one pair.

The underlying reason is that a Stellar pool is identified by a hash of its type,
its two assets in canonical order, and its fee, and the protocol admits exactly
one fee today. The 1308 rows above are the empirical form of that statement, and
they are what is recorded here, because the protocol rule is somebody else's
invariant and could be relaxed by a future protocol version without this
repository being told.


5. WHAT THE RECORDER DOES ABOUT IT, WHICH IS NOTHING
------------------------------------------------------

The version 2 recorder stores the response body of /liquidity_pools verbatim, as
a string, with its sha256. It does not count the records, does not filter them,
does not rank them and does not select one. If Horizon returns two records
tomorrow it will store two records tomorrow, and the file will say so.

That is not a convenience. CHOOSING AMONG CANDIDATE POOLS IS A METHODOLOGY
DECISION and methodology is the red zone. A recorder that quietly kept the
deepest pool would be answering that question every hour, in a way that leaves no
trace in the evidence and cannot be revised afterwards, because the pools it
dropped are not recoverable from Horizon for a past ledger.

The one place a multi-record response would be noticed today is
`poolReserves` in internal/horizon/decode.go, on the version 1 parsed path, which
is a different code path and unchanged by this work. FOR AL TO DECIDE, and stated
here rather than decided: if a pair ever does return more than one pool, what
does effective AMM depth mean across them. Summing reserves is wrong, because two
pools at different prices are not one deeper pool.
